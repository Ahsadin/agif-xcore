# Care-home paperwork software beats the baseline

**The consensus winner is L4-F1: software that helps German care homes get residents' social-assistance files complete. It launches in North Rhine-Westphalia (NRW), and the care home pays.** It is the only one of 15 finalists that all five evaluators rated TEST. Its mean score is **64.0 out of 100** (range 60.5–66.5), and four of the five evaluators would bet their €10,000 on it. **Nothing reached BUILD (75 or more).** The baseline, a daily automated check-in call for people aged 65+ living alone (BASE), was clearly beaten. Evaluators scored it **42.5–49.0 (mean 45.3), 15th of 16 ideas**, because VAT, churn and liability that cannot be capped break its €9.90 economics. L4-F1 beat BASE by 15 to 24 points on every evaluator's scorecard. Two runners-up sit on the TEST line with split votes: an audit-ready substrate log for biogas plants (L3-A, mean 60.1) and practice-run prevention plans for vets (L1-A, mean 59.5). The funnel was harsh: **120 raw ideas, 105 killed, 15 finalists.** Evaluators also marked the researchers' own finalist scores down by **7.8 points on average** once VAT, churn replacement and founder time were costed. Even the winner is a bet, not a plan. On the CFO evaluator's cash model it reaches €8,000 a month of run-rate profit in month 21 after launch, but only if one founder signs about four homes a month. The two evaluators who gave odds put success inside 24 months at 30% and 40%. The next step is a staged 90-day test capped at €5,000, gated first by a lawyer's opinion, with the biogas log as the switch option.

## A question-first method and a strict rulebook framed every idea

The study asked one question. Which non-AI app ideas could one hypothetical solo founder build and run that make lives measurably better and earn **at least €8,000 a month of profit after all business costs within 24 months, starting with at most €10,000**? Does any of them beat BASE?

Five researchers each searched one angle, called a lens. Lens 1 looked for business models proven elsewhere but missing here. Lens 2 looked at new legal obligations. Lens 3 looked at overlooked niche trades and organisations. Lens 4 looked at household pains where a third party has a money reason to pay. Lens 5 looked at new non-AI technical rails, such as instant payments and messaging APIs. Each lens started from a person's pain, not from a technology. Each killed most raw candidates, kept three finalists (IDs L1-A to L5-F3) and scored them provisionally, along with BASE.

Five evaluators then rescored all 15 finalists plus BASE, without seeing each other's files. They are hypothetical personas with different careers. **E1** is a bootstrapper who has sold one-person software firms. **E2** is a former venture investor and operating executive. **E3** is a sales and distribution realist. **E4** is a subscription-software CFO who built a 24-month cash model. **E5** is a former general counsel who has run a company. Each had 15 web searches. Several assumed the founder works in English and has no network in the target market, so they costed a native-speaking seller into German, Romanian and Swiss sales.

### Ten questions carry each idea from a pain to a kill test

Every finalist card answers the same ten questions in order. The table states what a complete answer contained, as the lenses applied it.

| Step | Question | What a complete answer contains |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Who suffers, when, how often, at what cost? | One sentence naming a concrete person and moment, plus sourced frequency and cost |
| Q2 | What is the one thing the app does? | A single deterministic job; everything else is out of scope |
| Q3 | Who already solves it? | A competitor table (name, country, price, what it does, gap), searched in the local language and abroad |
| Q4 | Why do people still suffer? | One or more root-cause classes, (a) to (g), with evidence |
| Q5 | Is it profitable? | Price, cost to serve, gross margin, fixed costs, customers needed = (€8,000 + fixed costs) ÷ (revenue per customer − cost to serve), reachable pool and share needed, CAC, churn, LTV and LTV/CAC |
| Q6 | How is it built? | MVP scope, stack and a week-by-week 12-week plan |
| Q7 | Where does it launch first? | A market chosen on evidence (gap verified, pool counted), not by default |
| Q8 | How does it make money? | Who pays, how much and how; the founder never holds client money |
| Q9 | How does one person run it? | Support load, automation, failure modes and fixes |
| Q10 | What did I miss? | Platform kill, incumbent copy, liability, false assumptions, churn, seasonality, and dated kill tests, usually to the first paying customers within 90 days for €2,000 or less |

The Q4 root-cause classes, as used across the notes, are: (a) too small a priority for funded companies; (b) language, local or regulatory fragmentation; (c) the sufferer is not the payer, or cannot or will not pay; (d) a new rule or rail only recently made it necessary or possible; (e) boring, unglamorous work; (f) the economics do not work; (g) it cannot be built as ordinary software. The lenses treated (f) and (g) as warning signs. If they explain the gap, the gap is not an opportunity.

### Eight hard rules filter, and ten weighted criteria rank

The rulebook below is the coordinator's exact wording, given identically to every researcher and evaluator. Violating any one hard rule kills an idea.

| Hard rule | Exact rule | Typical failure in this study |
|---|---|---|
| H1 No AI | Core value delivered by deterministic software (rules, schedules, records, forms, calculations, payments, telephony, messaging, integrations). No LLMs, machine learning, OCR, speech recognition or computer vision in the core value | BASE uses a keypad "press 1"; L3-A drops the OCR a rival uses |
| H2 Solo-buildable | One developer ships a sellable MVP in ≤ 12 weeks with standard web/mobile stacks and off-the-shelf APIs. No hardware manufacturing, inventory, vehicles or physical logistics | A tenant pack built on the EU Digital Identity Wallet, which is not yet live |
| H3 No licence-gated core | The founder must not need a banking, payment-institution, e-money, credit, insurance, legal-advice, tax-advice, medical-device or debt-collection licence to deliver the core value. Building on a licensed partner's API is allowed | Billing care insurers over the health-IT network (flagged as a risk) |
| H4 Platform-proof | Must not be made redundant within about 2 years by a free feature from Apple, Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, banks or the niche's dominant incumbent; must not depend on one revocable gatekeeper permission (scraping, accessibility APIs, unofficial APIs, one marketplace's goodwill) | Instant-payment checkout in Germany after banks launched Wero |
| H5 Real payer | Someone pays who directly feels the pain or has a direct financial reason to pay. No ad-funded, data-selling or "monetise later" models | A moving-house concierge paid by selling switching leads |
| H6 Ethics | No interest-bearing lending or BNPL, gambling or speculative trading, alcohol/tobacco/adult content, surveillance of people without their own consent, dark patterns, or reselling user data | A tool paid by success fee when disabled employees disclose their status |
| H7 No cold-start marketplace | No two-sided marketplace that needs liquidity on both sides before it delivers value | Night-shift childcare matching for nurses |
| H8 Not crowded | Not 3 or more funded or clearly profitable competitors already selling the same thing in the chosen launch market at a similar price, unless there is a specific wedge they structurally cannot copy | Self-managed condominium software in Germany |

| Criterion | Weight | What earns a high score |
|---|---|---|
| C1 Pain | 10 | Frequent, costly, evidenced pain |
| C2 Willingness to pay and payer clarity | 15 | A clear payer with a direct money reason and price evidence |
| C3 Gap realness | 10 | Verified absence of a good solution, with a reason |
| C4 Distribution | 15 | A cheap, named, verified channel to buyers |
| C5 Unit economics | 15 | High margin and LTV/CAC; evaluators treated gross margin under 70% or LTV/CAC under 3 as failing marks |
| C6 Market sufficiency | 5 | The goal needs only a small share of the reachable pool |
| C7 Solo operability | 10 | Low support and operating load for one person |
| C8 Durability | 10 | Hard to copy; real switching costs |
| C9 Risk (inverse) | 5 | Low legal, liability and reputational exposure |
| C10 Speed to proof | 5 | A money signal within about 90 days for €2,000 or less |

Each criterion is scored 1–10. The weighted score is Σ(score × weight) ÷ 10, so the maximum is 100. A score of **75 or more is BUILD, 60–74 is TEST, and under 60 is KILL. Any hard-rule violation is KILL.** Evaluators flagged an idea "at risk" when a credible mechanism could break a hard rule but the evidence did not prove it under the rule's exact wording. An "at risk" flag is a watch item, not a violation.

### The baseline and the goal

BASE is a daily automated call to a person aged 65+ who lives alone in Germany without a care grade. The person presses a key to confirm they are fine; a missed check-in alerts the family, who pay €9.90 a month. The brief assumed about €3 of cost per customer and about 1,000 paying families to reach the goal. It cited a US analogue at $14.99 and German home-alarm (Hausnotruf) services at €27–40. The lenses scored BASE at 51.0–57.5. E4 read "profit" as before personal income tax, with the founder's living costs outside the €10,000.

### How evidence is labelled in this report

A linked claim is a fact that a researcher or evaluator confirmed from a search-engine extract of the linked page. Nobody could open full pages (see the evidence-limits section). "Judgement" marks an evaluator's unverified opinion or estimate. "Model" marks output of E4's 24-month cash model, which counts months from a January 2027 launch after a 12-week build. "Statute, not fetched" marks E5's reading of a law that counsel must confirm. All scores are out of 100.

## The care-home case manager is the only unanimous TEST

L4-F1 (lens 4's "Heimkosten-Lotse") gives a care home and a resident's family one shared case file. The file covers the paperwork that unlocks public money when a resident's savings run out: social assistance for care (Hilfe zur Pflege), NRW's state subsidy for a home's investment costs (Pflegewohngeld) and housing benefit. The home pays. Its criterion means show the shape of the bet: pain 8.0 and market sufficiency 8.6 are strong; distribution 5.2, solo operability 5.0 and risk 4.6 are the weak spots.

### Q1–Q2: Homes pre-finance €3,100 or more a month per resident while files sit incomplete

When a resident's savings run out, the family must gather dozens of income and asset documents for the local social-welfare office (Sozialamt), while the home keeps billing. The average first-year resident co-payment was **€3,108 a month** nationally on 1 July 2025 ([vdek](https://www.vdek.com/presse/pressemitteilungen/2025/pflegeheim-monatliche-eigenbeteiligung-steigt-stationaere-pflege.html)) and **€3,427 in NRW** ([vdek NRW](https://www.vdek.com/LVen/NRW/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2025/stationaere-pflege-nrw.html)). In a 2025 ARD survey, about **27% of answering offices needed 6–12 months** to decide, and about 5% more than 12. Homes are paid only after approval, so residents risk losing their place ([Ärzteblatt](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/news/antragsstau-zur-pflege-in-sozialamtern-affb0712-f301-4506-b133-ea19da7c2365)).

The payer's pain is quantified by a June 2026 PwC survey of more than 100 homes. **55% name the Sozialamt as their largest debtor, one in five has €100,000 or more outstanding, staff spend 8.3 hours a week on open items, and 46% have no standard claims process** ([PwC](https://www.pwc.de/de/pressemitteilungen/2026/fehlende-liquiditaet-in-pflegeheimen-gefaehrdet-die-versorgung.html)). Of the homes whose largest debtor is the Sozialamt, **60% blame missing documents** ([Apotheken Umschau](https://www.apotheken-umschau.de/gesundheitspolitik/pflegeheime-warten-monatelang-auf-hilfe-zur-pflege-von-sozialaemtern-1539809.html)). That is a cause deterministic software can attack. Net spending on this aid reached €5.3bn in 2024, up 17.7% ([Destatis via presseportal](https://www.presseportal.de/pm/32102/6098431)). In Berlin, applications for people in a care home still go on paper by post ([service.berlin.de](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/324474/pdf/)).

The one thing the app does is give each resident one shared case file. Rules based on income, assets and guardianship tell the family exactly which documents to upload. The file sends a date-securing informal notice on day one. It tracks every follow-up request from the office and every renewal date; NRW's Pflegewohngeld is paid directly to the home and approved for 12 months at a time ([recht.nrw.de](https://recht.nrw.de/system/files/BH/5196-21888.htm)). It also shows the home one board of open receivables per payer. **The life improvement is measurable in the units the home pays for:** days from first contact to a complete file, and months of aid secured. Families get entitled support sooner, and residents are less exposed to losing their place.

### Q3–Q4: No one serves the home's side of the file

Lens 4 and all five evaluators searched separately for software on the home's side of this workflow. **None found any** ([SoftGuide directory](https://www.softguide.de/software/pflegeheime); [betanet](https://www.betanet.de/hilfe-zur-pflege.html)). Large operators hand out paper checklists and model letters instead ([Agaplesion](https://www.hdv.agaplesion.de/fileadmin/Agaplesion_hdv-darmstadt/Infomaterial/Checkliste_Sozialhilfetraeger_AGA_WuP_web.pdf)). The only funded German player, Myosotis, sells its "formfix" application aid to welfare offices, not to homes. It raised a Series A of €10.5–12m in February 2024, led by TVM with €8m ([startbase](https://www.startbase.de/news/105-millionen-euro-fuer-myosotis/); [TVM](https://tvm-capital.com/?p=6430)). It also claims 400+ customers in DACH and England for a separate app that connects homes with relatives (vendor claim, [job posting](https://join.com/companies/myo/16523373-mitarbeiter-in-sozialhilfe-hilfe-zur-pflege-m-w-d)). That app is the most credible copy threat, because it already sits inside homes. Factoring firms fix the cash side, typically advancing 80–90% of an invoice ([Agicap](https://agicap.com/de/artikel/factoring/)), but they do not fix missing documents. Abroad, at least four US vendors sell "Medicaid pending" receivable tracking to nursing homes ([SNF Metrics](https://snfmetrics.com/ar-tracker-dashboard/); [ExaCare](https://www.exacare.com/article/medicaid-pending-nursing-homes)), which shows the model works. One gap was checked by nobody: whether care-home ERP systems such as Medifox DAN or Connext Vivendi already chase family documents.

The root cause is mainly class (c). The family suffers but will not buy back-office software. The party that could fix the process is a slow public buyer, and the funded vendor sells to it. Fragmentation (b) adds to this: the ARD survey wrote to 478 offices, and Pflegewohngeld exists only in some states ([Verbraucherzentrale NRW](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.nrw/wissen/gesundheit-pflege/pflege-im-heim/pflegewohngeld-in-nrw-finanzielle-hilfe-in-stationaeren-einrichtungen-86793)). The work is unglamorous (e). It is not (f), because homes already pay for factoring. It is not (g), because everything is ordinary web software.

### Q5: About 65 homes at €179 a month reach the goal in roughly 21 months

| Metric | Lens 4 claim | Evaluators' recomputation |
|---|---|---|
| Price | €129 / €179 / €249 a month by bed band; average €179, net (business buyer) | Same on all five cards |
| Cost to serve per home | €18 | €18–23.90 (E4 adds support time at €35 an hour) |
| Gross margin | ~90% | 87–90% |
| Fixed costs per month | €1,800 | €1,100–3,000 (E2 includes a €1,200 German-speaking seller) |

| Monthly churn | 1.5% | 1.2–1.5% |
| LTV/CAC | ~21 | 7.8–15; E1 2.3–3.5 on a loaded basis |
| Homes needed for €8,000 | 61 | 61–69, about 0.5–1.5% of the pool |
| Time to €8,000 run-rate | not stated | ~18–28 months; E4 model: month 21 base case, month 23 with annual prepayment, month 32 under stress |

Sales capacity, not market size, sets the timeline. The goal needs only a sliver of the 11,250 long-term-care homes ([Curacon/Destatis](https://www.curacon.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Curacon/Downloads/Produktbeispiel_sAHBenchmark_Max.pdf)). But a founder closing about four homes a month needs about 20 months after launch; at three a month it slips to about 28 (E3, judgement). E4's model shows the cash cost of speed. The base case dips to about **€1,100 of cash in month 7**. Selling annual prepayment instead of a discounted €99 pilot keeps cash above **€6,600** throughout and reaches €8,000 two months later, in month 23 (model). E4's stress case, with 2% churn and three new homes a month, reaches it only in month 32. Because model months start at a January 2027 launch, month 21 is about September 2028, just inside 24 months from today. The value check is sound. Cutting the 8.3 weekly hours by a quarter saves about 9 hours a month, worth about €315 at €35 an hour, before counting earlier cash. So €179 a month returns at least twice its cost (E4, judgement on lens 4's assumption).

One pricing question no evaluator checked: can a care home reclaim VAT on a software fee? If it cannot, the home's real cost is about €213 a month (€179 × 1.19). A tax adviser should answer this before pricing.

### Q6–Q9: A 12-week build, an NRW launch and a German-speaking seller

**Q6, build.** Lens 4's MVP has nine parts. They are a resident case file with a map of all payers, a rules-based document checklist, a family portal with magic-link login and uploads, the day-one notice with a proof-of-sending log, form filling for the most common NRW forms, a follow-up tracker, a renewal engine, a receivables board with dunning templates, and a monthly PDF report. The stack is off the shelf: Django or Rails with Postgres, PDF form filling, transactional e-mail and SMS, encrypted storage hosted in Germany, and SEPA billing through GoCardless or Stripe. OCR, AI, financing, legal opinions and ERP integration are out of scope. The 12 weeks run from discovery calls through the form library, renewal engine and security work (encryption, two-factor login, audit log, data-processing agreement, impact-assessment template) to two or three pilots.

Evaluators changed the emphasis, not the scope. Some districts already accept online uploads ([RBK FAQ](https://www.rbk-direkt.de/download/429/hilfe-zur-pflege-in-einem-pflegeheim---fragen-und-antworten)), so form filling is commoditising (E4). The responsible office is usually the one for the resident's address before admission ([Schwalm-Eder-Kreis](https://schwalm-eder-kreis.de/Home/Barrierefreiheit.htm/Aemter/50-4-SGB-XII-Hilfe-zur-Pflege.html)), so a form library would spill beyond NRW (E5). Both point the same way: lead with document chasing, follow-ups, renewals and receivables, and keep forms thin. E3 adds a design rule: if relatives ignore the portal, the home's administrator must be able to run the case alone.

**Q7, launch.** All five evaluators call NRW an evidence-driven choice. It has among the highest co-payments. Pflegewohngeld is paid directly to homes and renewed every year, so the payer has a recurring workflow tied to its revenue. The German problem is quantified by recent independent sources, and the US version is already served.

**Q8, money.** A subscription per home by bed band (€129, €179 or €249 a month) paid by SEPA direct debit, with annual prepayment earning two months free. Lens 4 proposed a three-month paid pilot at €99 a month. There are no success fees, which would reward pushing residents into aid, and no financing products.

**Q9, operation.** Lens 4 estimates 2–4 support tickets per home per month, or 10–20 hours a month at 60 homes, plus about a day a month of form checks (assumption). Evaluators added a German-speaking seller or onboarding freelancer at €400–1,200 a month, an external data-protection officer, cyber insurance and a data-protection impact assessment. E1 thinks support and security are the real cost for one person and scores solo operability 4 out of 10. A breach of residents' financial and care data would be existential for a one-person company (judgement).

### Q10: Legal design, slow buyers and one funded copier are the risks

The consensus top risk is legal design and ethics. It covers the German Legal Services Act (RDG), who may represent a resident before the office, special-category health data under GDPR Article 9, and the conflict between a home that wants to be paid and a family that may want to protect assets. The second risk is distribution: cold calls in German to busy administrators, with chains buying centrally. E3 expects 6–12-month buying cycles in chains and 1–3 months in independent homes (judgement), which is why every evaluator starts with independent operators of one to three homes. The third is copying by Myosotis or an ERP vendor, plus policy change.

Lens 4's kill tests survived evaluation. By week 6, at least 15 NRW administrators must be interviewed, and at least half must report three or more cases open for longer than three months. By day 90, at least five homes must pay at least €99 a month, or sign letters of intent with a price. Within three months of pilots, follow-up requests or days to a complete file must fall by at least 30%. The founder stops if Myosotis offers its tool free and pilots switch. A counsel check of the RDG and GDPR model comes first, narrowing the product to a staff-only tool if needed.

### Where evaluators disagreed, and which side the evidence favours

**Is the day-one notice a moat or a must-have?** E1, E3 and E4 call the date-securing notice the strongest single feature. A 2024 ruling of the Baden-Württemberg state social court held that even an incomplete application fixes the date from which the office knows of the need ([gegen-hartz](https://www.gegen-hartz.de/urteile/sozialhilfe-formloser-antrag-sichert-anspruch-auf-hilfe-zur-pflege)). E2 counters that an informal application by phone or e-mail is already enough in Salzgitter ([Salzgitter leaflet](https://salzgitter.de/soziales/downloads/hilfe-zur-pflege/Merkblatt-Hilfe-zur-Pflege-stationaer.pdf)). E5 adds that care aid starts when the office knows of the need (§18 SGB XII), while old-age basic income needs a formal application (§44 SGB XII) (statute, not fetched). **The evidence favours E2 and E5.** Because the rule is permissive, the notice is a cheap must-have, not a moat. The lasting value lies in complete documents, follow-ups, renewals and receivables.

**Is policy a threat or a tailwind?** Lens 4 feared a cap on co-payments would shrink caseloads. E5 found a draft reform, presented on 3 June 2026 and planned for 1 January 2027, that would delay the full 75% co-payment subsidy from 36 to 54 months. That means more residents needing aid ([check24](https://www.check24.de/pflegeversicherung/pflegereform/); [gegen-hartz](https://www.gegen-hartz.de/news/3-245-euro-fuers-pflegeheim-ab-2027-zahlen-rentner-laenger-selbst)). A cap at €1,500 is so far only a political demand ([AOK](https://www.aok.de/pp/gg/update/pflegereform-und-eigenanteile/)). **The evidence favours E5 for the next two years.** Neither change is law yet, and a cap stays a long-term risk.

**Is one person enough to run it?** E1 scores solo operability 4, E4 scores it 6, and the rest score 5. Both sides rest on assumed ticket counts, so no evidence settles it yet. The 90-day plan measures it directly.

**Is the pool shrinking?** More than 1,000 care facilities are reported closed since 2023, an unofficial employers' figure ([Business Punk](https://www.business-punk.com/pflege-kollaps-in-deutschland-ueber-1000-heime-dicht/)). Against 11,250 long-term-care homes and a need for about 65, this does not threaten market sufficiency.

### L3-A: a rich payer for biogas evidence, in a gap that is closing

The practice collects by SEPA through its own payment account, and the app keeps an entitlement ledger and sends reminders.

**Q3–Q4.** Proof abroad is the strongest in the study. CVS's UK Healthy Pet Club had **519,000 members** in mid-2025 ([FCA RNS](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/5850272.html)). ### L2-F1: a live EU cyber duty in a field filling with free tools

Its untested number is how many micro makers will pay before enforcement, while free and $29 tools exist.

**Q1–Q2.** Since **11 September 2026**, makers of connected products sold in the EU must report actively exploited vulnerabilities under the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA). They owe an early warning within 24 hours, a notification within 72 hours and a final report within 14 days of a fix being available ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202402847); [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting)). From 11 December 2027 they also need CE marking, technical documentation, a software bill of materials (SBOM) and a declared support period. Products already on the market are in scope for reporting ([cyberresilienceact.eu](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/es/news/legacy-products-in-scope-cra-reporting-11-september-2026.html)). The app keeps each product's SBOM current and matches it daily against public vulnerability feeds. It runs the reporting clocks with copy-ready drafts for ENISA's portal, which has no API ([ENISA](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/the-cra-single-reporting-platform-is-launched)), and generates the paperwork.

**Q3–Q4.** Lens 2 found only sbomify at a comparable price ($159 a month, [sbomify](https://sbomify.com/pricing/)). Evaluators contradicted that. CRALedger sells a $29-a-month plan that tracks Article 14 deadlines ([pickyourapp](https://pickyourapp.com/products/craledger-duty-records)). CRA Evidence lists from €1 ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com.au/software/2141675/cra-evidence)). Verimu started billing in September 2026 ([Verimu](https://verimu.com/)). EU-funded free tools for small firms are arriving, including the CRACoWi wizard with firmware SBOM extraction ([CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101225583)) and Eclipse's OCCTET ([ADTmag](https://adtmag.com/articles/2025/08/20/eclipse-foundation-launches-project-to-aid-cyber-resilience-act-compliance.aspx)). The root causes are (d) a new rule and (a) a niche too small for funded firms. The gap is real but closing within months.

### Why twelve finalists fell below the TEST line

Four corrections recur across the evaluators' work, and they explain most of the 7.8-point drop. **First, VAT.** All five evaluators treated German consumer prices as including 19% VAT, so €9.90 is €8.32 and €4.99 is €4.19 net. VAT-exempt teachers and associations cannot reclaim it either. **Second, churn replacement.** E3 and E4 counted the marketing needed just to replace lost customers: customers needed = (€8,000 + fixed costs) ÷ (net revenue − cost to serve − monthly churn × CAC). For consumer ideas this multiplies the requirement two to five times (E4). **Third, founder time.** At €40–50 an hour, founder-led selling of 15–40 hours per account turns lens LTV/CAC ratios of 10–30 into 2–5 (E1). **Fourth, realistic churn.** ChartMogul's benchmark puts median monthly churn at 6.1% for products under $25 a month and 2.2% above $500 ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/blog/good-customer-churn-rate/)). For low-priced products that is far above the 0.8–3% several lenses assumed.

Evaluators also found rivals the lenses missed in six places. ## The baseline's €9.90 economics collapse after VAT, churn and liability

BASE was not a straw man. All five evaluators confirmed its gap: none found a German phone-only daily check-in service. But every correction cut against it.

| Item | Brief or lens assumption | Evaluator correction | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net price | €9.90 a month | €8.32 after 19% VAT | All five; recomputation |
| Gross margin | ~70% | 60–70% (E3 ~60%, E5 64%, E4 68%) | Recomputation |
| Monthly churn | not stated | 3–5% | ChartMogul's 6.1% median for products under $25 a month, before deaths and moves into care |
| LTV/CAC | 2–3 in the lenses | 0.6–2.8 | Below the 3× mark on every scorecard |
| Families needed for €8,000 | about 1,000 | 1,550–1,911 before acquisition costs; 3,000–6,900 counting churn replacement (E3); 5,460 (E4) | Recomputation |
| Home-alarm price anchor | €27–40 a month | about €20 for basic tariffs | [web.de](https://web.de/magazine/ratgeber/kind-familie/notrufsysteme-senioren-moeglichkeiten-34479312) |
| Cash over 24 months | not modelled | €10,000 gone around month 8; −€9,900 by month 24 | E4 model |

The brief's "about 1,000 families" fails even before VAT. 1,000 × (€9.90 − €3) is €6,900 a month of gross margin, already short of €8,000 before any fixed costs (E3). E4's optimistic case, with €40 CAC and 3% churn, still needs about 2,000 families and makes only about €400 a month of run-rate profit by month 24 (model). E3 estimates the price would need to rise to about €19.90 for the model to work.

**Telephony route costs matter.** The €3 cost per customer holds only with calls routed from inside the European Economic Area. Twilio lists German calls at about $0.028 a minute to landlines and $0.042 a minute to mobiles from an EEA origin, but about $0.376 a minute to mobiles at the general rate ([Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/voice/pricing/de)). At the general rate, 30 billed minutes a month to a mobile would cost more than the net price (E4).

**Liability cannot be capped.** In E5's reading, German consumer terms cannot exclude or limit liability for death or injury caused by negligence, or for any damage caused by gross negligence ([§309 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__309.html); statute, not fetched). The senior or their heirs might also claim as third parties protected by the family's contract (judgement). The service must never be marketed as an emergency service, and it needs the senior's own consent, or a legal representative's if the senior lacks capacity (H6).

**Demand abroad is weak.** The US analogues are mostly free public programmes, such as Maryland's Senior Call-Check ([Maryland](https://aging.prod.maryland.gov/senior-call-check)). A trade headline reports that enrolment in check-in services for seniors remains low ([HealthExec](https://healthexec.com/topics/patient-care/precision-medicine/enrollment-check-services-seniors-remains-low)). Police-run daily-call programmes struggle with attrition ([newsaegis](https://newsaegis.com/?p=48162)). A Dutch service charged €65 a month for a daily human call in 2015 ([DutchNews](https://www.dutchnews.nl/2015/06/for-e65-a-daily-phone-call-to-mum-made-by-someone-else)). As E5 put it, BASE is not a bad cause. Its life-safety promise needs 24/7 reliability that a one-person company on a 64% margin cannot fund.

### (b) In Switzerland the channel problem crossed the border, but the crowding did not

Germany has 1,771,291 condominium owners' associations ([EWiR](https://ewir.jura.uni-koeln.de/sites/ewir/user_upload/Praesentation_Storm.pdf)). ## Search extracts, not full documents, carry most of the evidence

Every number in this report passed through a narrow pipe. The session's network policy blocked direct access to most primary sources. Lens 2 could not reach EUR-Lex, GOV.UK, legislation.gov.uk, the FDA, the Federal Register, Spain's official gazette or ENISA, so it read official texts only through search extracts. Lens 3 found page fetching blocked for almost every domain, including the Bundestag lobby register, gesetze-im-internet.de, topagrar and NZZ. Lenses 4 and 5 hit DNS failures and proxy refusals. All ten page fetches the evaluators attempted failed. **"Verified" in these notes therefore means "confirmed by a search-engine extract that attributes the fact to that URL".** Nobody opened the full page. Anyone relying on an exact figure should re-read it at source.

Search budgets ran out. Lens 2's shared budget was exhausted before it could check payment fees, hosting prices and several dates, so 15 of its kill rows rest wholly on unverified prior knowledge. Lens 3 could not research beekeepers, volunteer fire brigades, music associations, English village halls and parish councils, or US niches. Lens 5 ran out after about 31 searches, so the butcher pre-order idea reached evaluation without any competitor or market research. Lens 1's kills 8–13 rest on background reasoning. Each evaluator had 15 searches and spent them on the claims most likely to flip a verdict. Many checks remain undone, including care-home ERP features, works-council software with representative modules, daycare apps with duty rosters, maker apps with heat-pump diagnosis, battery-passport prices for small firms and housing-ERP notices.

**Competitor funding is mostly unknown.** The crowding rule (H8) needs three funded or clearly profitable rivals. Nobody could establish funding or profitability for Stabley, EquineM, EinfachSTWEG, LIMMOBI, Nabisy Software, TerraVis, Biotaktix, agriportance, Caty, Zarina, the white-label butcher-app vendor, sbomify, Verimu, CRA Evidence or CRALedger. Other limits bear on specific verdicts. Several inputs are old: horse and boarding counts date from 2001–2017, the Berlin parent-run daycare count from 2019, the EEG diary rules from a 2019 presentation, and payment-provider prices from 2015 or undated sources. ## Conclusion

The sharpest lesson is about shape, not sector. Every idea an evaluator bet on or named as a second choice sells a deterministic workflow for roughly €60–250 a month to a business that loses visible money when the workflow fails. E4 drew the other half of the line: every idea it refused sells reassurance or optimisation for under €10 a month to consumers. The other evaluators' refusals add one more failure shape, a one-person company guaranteeing other firms' legal duties for years (L2-F3). BASE fails for the consumer reason, not because daily check-ins are worthless. After VAT, churn replacement and paid acquisition, nothing is left to fund the 24/7 reliability its promise implies, and German consumer law will not let a founder cap the liability. The winner also shows how "measurably better lives" and profit can share one metric. Days to a complete file is the home's cash, the family's aid and the founder's proof of value at once, and that alignment is what keeps the ethics manageable.

The second lesson is about method. Single-researcher scores ran 7.8 points hot, mostly through four omissions (VAT, churn replacement, founder time and a native-language seller) and one habit: treating "not found" as "absent". A second, independent competitor search shrank the gap in six places. Anyone reusing this rulebook should build those corrections into Q5 and require a second searcher on Q3 before scoring. Market sufficiency was the strongest criterion and distribution and durability among the weakest, so a solo founder's real constraint is selling capacity: about four high-touch closes a month, in E4's judgement. The filter that follows, in my synthesis, is simple. Pick a payer worth €150 or more a month, need no more than about 70 of them, and prove the channel for €5,000 before building anything beyond a concierge.
