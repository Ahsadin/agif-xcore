## (2) Per-idea notes

**How I recomputed unit economics (same method for every idea)**
- **Net prices.** B2C prices exclude VAT. For B2B buyers who cannot reclaim VAT (e.g., VAT-exempt teachers), I use the net share of the gross price.
- **Two "customers needed" figures:**
  - *Naive* = (€8,000 + fixed costs) ÷ gross margin per customer. This is what the lenses used.
  - *Steady state* = (€8,000 + fixed costs) ÷ (gross margin per customer − monthly churn × CAC). This counts the acquisition spend needed to replace churned customers, which is a real business cost.
- **Time to goal.** Months after launch to reach the steady-state count, given a stated number of new customers per month (gross adds) and the churn rate. The MVP adds about 3 months before launch.
- **CAC.** Cash cost only. Founder hours are listed separately. For German or Romanian outbound sales I include a freelance native-speaking seller at about €25–35/hour (JUDGEMENT), because the founder works in English.

## (4) My €10,000 bet and my never-fund pick

### Bet: L4-F1, care-home financing case manager (NRW)

**Why this idea**
1. **The biggest, best-evidenced money reason in the set.**
   - Homes pre-finance €3.1–3.4k per resident per month during delays ([vdek](https://www.vdek.com/presse/pressemitteilungen/2025/pflegeheim-monatliche-eigenbeteiligung-steigt-stationaere-pflege.html)).
   - 1 in 5 homes carries ≥€100k of open receivables, and homes spend 8.3 staff hours a week on open items ([PwC 2026](https://www.pwc.de/de/pressemitteilungen/2026/fehlende-liquiditaet-in-pflegeheimen-gefaehrdet-die-versorgung.html)).
   - 60% of affected homes blame missing documents ([Apotheken Umschau](https://www.apotheken-umschau.de/gesundheitspolitik/pflegeheime-warten-monatelang-auf-hilfe-zur-pflege-von-sozialaemtern-1539809.html)). That is a fixable, deterministic cause.
2. **The fewest customers needed (about 69 homes)** at the highest revenue per customer (about €179/month). That is the only shape where a slow, expensive B2B channel still reaches €8k/month within about 20–28 months.
3. **No seasonality**, unlike L3-A (biogas) and L5-F2 (butchers).
4. **The funded player sells to the other side.** Myosotis sells formfix to welfare offices, not homes, and my directory search found no home-side tool.

**How I would stage the €10k**

| Weeks | Spend | Activities | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–6 | ≤€2,000 | Fixed-fee counsel on the Legal Services Act and GDPR (about €1,000); a German freelance seller for 40 h (about €1,200) to book 15 discovery calls with independent NRW homes; sell the concierge "open-cases audit". | ≥5 paid audits or LOIs with a price, **and** ≥50% of homes report ≥3 cases open for more than 3 months (lens 4's KT1). |
| 7–18 | ≤€5,000 | Build the MVP (founder time); continue the seller (about €2,500); demo travel (about €800); DPIA and processing-agreement templates (about €500); hosting and tools (about €500). | ≥5 homes paying ≥€99/month. |
| 19–30 | ≤€3,000 | Publish a case study; walk a care trade fair; sign 1–2 referral partners (factoring firms or consultancies). | ≥15 paying homes and ≥3 closes per month. |

**Runner-up: L3-A (biogas).** It has a better-verified channel: FvB, about 5,000 members, 23–26 regional groups. It is my second choice because:
- only two selling seasons fit inside 24 months;
- four small adjacent vendors are already in the field;
- it needs about 3× as many customers (about 205 plants).

### Never fund: L2-F3, Battery Passport Lite

- **The product makes the founder a single point of failure for customers' legal right to sell batteries.** Passports must stay available for years, and lens 2 itself rates this liability "high for a solo founder". No amount of growth fixes that structure.
- **The addressable pool is tiny and unverified.**
  - Lens 2 assumes 1–3k EU finishers, which means 2–7% of the pool is needed.
  - Large drive and battery suppliers will issue passports for the batteries they complete (JUDGEMENT), removing the best customers.
- **The market is crowded and still moving.** Digital-product-passport vendors are numerous, and standards and the registry are still changing.
- **Honourable mentions:**
  - L4-F3: distribution is effectively impossible within 24 months (about 1 close per month, 6–12-month committee cycles).
  - BASE: missed-alert liability, plus about 60% gross margin and LTV/CAC below 3.
