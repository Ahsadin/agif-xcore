# Lens 1 — "Proven elsewhere, missing here": non-AI app ideas one solo founder can build and run

Research date: 2026-10-12. Scope: business models with proven paying demand in one country that are missing or weakly served in another large, wealthy market. The METHOD (Q1–Q10) and RULEBOOK (H1–H8, C1–C10) were applied exactly as given. Searches were run in English, German, French and Dutch through a web-search tool plus app-store and software-directory pages.

"Missing" means **not found** in local-language web searches and software directories. That is not proof of absence (see the Gaps sections). Company announcements to regulators (RNS) are labelled "company disclosure". Vendor pages and vendor-linked listings are labelled "vendor claim".

> **Bottom line.** I generated 16 raw candidates. 13 were killed and 3 finalists remain, all launching in Germany:
> - **(A)** GOT-compliant monthly preventive-care plans for independent vet practices. Provisional score **67.0 (TEST)**, clearly above the baseline.
> - **(B)** Boarding-stable billing, extras logging and arena booking. **60.5 (TEST, marginal)**.
> - **(C)** Lesson admin and SEPA billing for private music teachers. **57.0 (KILL band)**.
>
> I scored the BASELINE at **51.0**. If the evaluators score the baseline at 61 or higher, only (A) beats it. At 58 or higher, (C) no longer beats it. **Only (A) is a strong "beats the baseline" candidate.**

### Takeaway
Most foreign models I checked have already crossed into the obvious target market:
- French pool-service software is crowded.
- German dog-daycare software exists.

### Inferences
- The best "missing here" gaps sit where a **local rule blocks a straight copy** of the foreign model. Germany's GOT fee floor is the example. The foreign incumbent then has to rebuild for one country, which a solo founder can do first.
- Ideas whose foreign success depends on a **channel that does not exist locally** fail C4 even when the gap is real. Court-ordered co-parenting apps are the example.

### Gaps
- Kills #8–#13 rest on background knowledge. They were not re-verified with searches this session, so treat them as provisional.
- I searched app stores through web search, not by browsing native store catalogues per country.

### Finalist A — "GOT-konformer Vorsorgeplan": white-label monthly preventive-care plans for independent small-animal vet practices in Germany

#### Q1 — Who suffers, when, how often, and what it costs
**One sentence:** A German dog or cat owner gets hit two or three times a year by lumpy prevention bills (vaccines, check-up, parasite control), and those bills rose sharply after the 2022 vet fee reform. Owners delay or skip care, and the independent practice loses those visits, has no recurring revenue and is losing clients to chains.

Evidence:
- **Fees rose.** In a Deutscher Tierschutzbund survey of animal shelters (April 2026), vet fees were 22% higher on average. Almost 80% of shelters said their costs had risen 30–50% since 2022, and more owners are asking shelters for help with vet bills ([t-online](https://www.t-online.de/nachrichten/panorama/tiere/id_101219134/hoehere-tierarztkosten-belasten-tierheime.html)).
- **Owners cut back.** In an Osnabrück master's survey of 14,021 horse owners, 40.3% said they use vet services less often because of the higher GOT fees ([web.de](https://web.de/magazine/wissen/tiere/umfrage-got-pferdehalter-rufen-seltener-tierarzt-40637804)). The sample is self-selected and covers horses only.
- **Unexpected bills hurt.** A YouGov poll reported via KNA found that an unexpectedly high vet bill would financially burden two-thirds of dog and cat owners. Source as reported in [leinetal24](https://www.leinetal24.de/verbraucher/umfrage-mehr-katzen-als-hunde-tierarzt-kosten-steigen-zr-94433587.html); I did not confirm the exact article (see Gaps).
- **No German data on skipped visits.** Allianz notes there is no systematic German data on owners postponing vet visits. A UK survey found 9% had postponed necessary visits for financial reasons ([Allianz](https://www.allianz.de/gesundheit/tierkrankenversicherung/studie-tierarztkosten/)).
- **Scale.** 33.4m pets live in German households, including 15.7m cats and about 10m dogs; 43% of households keep pets ([IVH/ZZF, April 2026](https://www.zzf.de/fileadmin/ZZF/Pressemeldungen/2026/2026_04_27_Marktdaten_der_Heimtierbranche/IVH_ZZF_PM_Der_Deutsche_Heimtiermarkt_2025.pdf)).
- **Cost of prevention (France as an analog).** Routine care runs €150–300 a year, plus €100–200 for vaccines and antiparasitics (vendor article, [SantéVet](https://www.santevet.com/articles/plans-de-prevention-le-budget-sante-animale-maitrise-1)). No German equivalent figure was found.

#### Q2 — The one thing the app does
It lets an independent practice launch and run a **GOT-compliant monthly prevention plan** in one afternoon:
- a plan builder whose fees can never go below the GOT 1× rate
- online enrolment with a SEPA mandate through the practice's own payment account
- front-desk tracking of which included services have been used
- automated reminders for due vaccinations and deworming
- cancellation and year-end settlement statements

#### Q4 — Why German owners still suffer, and the root-cause class
**Primary class: (b) language / local / regulatory fragmentation. Secondary: (a) too small a priority for the funded incumbents.**
- **The German fee schedule blocks a straight copy.** Under § 1(2) GOT, fees below the single (1×) rate are only allowed in the cases of § 5(1). Under § 5(1), going below 1× must be agreed **in text form, in a justified individual case, before** the service is provided. The only exception without an agreement is neutering feral cats ([GOT 2022, gesetze-im-internet](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/got_2022/BJNR140100022.html)). Older commentary adds that a low animal value or local custom never justifies going below 1× ([GOT 2017 commentary PDF, pre-2024](https://www.agila.de/images/pdf/GOT2017-07.pdf)).
- **So the UK "10% off everything" plans cannot be copied as they are.** A German plan must price every included service at ≥1× GOT. Any member discount must stop at the 1× floor, which is my inference from those two sections.
- **The incumbents look elsewhere.** PVA chose Spain for its 2025 continental expansion. IDEXX sells Petly in the US only.
- **This is not (f).** Plan economics are proven at scale in the UK.
- **This is not (g).** The product is ordinary web software on SEPA rails.

#### Q6 — How to build it
**MVP scope:**
- Practice and staff accounts.
- Plan builder: GOT item numbers, a multiplier enforced at ≥1.0, and a member-discount calculator with a "GOT floor guard".
- Per-practice enrolment page with QR code.
- Plan terms generated as PDF, including a cancellation button for online consumer contracts.
- SEPA mandate and collection through the practice's own GoCardless or Stripe Connect account. The founder never holds client money.
- Entitlement ledger: one click at the front desk to mark a service used.
- Rule-based due-date reminders by e-mail and SMS.
- Failed-debit handling via webhooks.
- Cancellation and year-end settlement statement: services used at plan prices versus amounts paid.
- Dashboard (active plans, MRR, churn) and CSV/DATEV-friendly export.

**Stack:** TypeScript (Next.js) or Rails, Postgres, GoCardless or Stripe Connect, Postmark for e-mail, a German SMS gateway, Gotenberg for PDFs, EU hosting (e.g. Hetzner). Integrating with practice-management software is out of scope for the MVP.

**Design note for H6:** the plan is a fixed-fee 12-month **service subscription with no interest or fees**. Entitlements can optionally accrue only as payments arrive, so the model never becomes deferred-payment credit (see Gaps).

#### Q8 — How it makes money
- **Who pays:** the practice (B2B).
- **Pricing:** €2.50 per active plan per month with a €99 minimum after the ramp. Optional €290 onboarding after the first 20 practices (ASSUMPTION).
- **How they pay:** a monthly SEPA debit or card through the founder's own Stripe Billing or GoCardless. Annual prepayment earns two months free.
- **Owner payments** go straight from owner to practice and never pass through the founder. This keeps the founder outside payment-institution licensing (H3).

#### Q9 — How one person runs it
- **Support load:** about 1–2 hours of onboarding per practice plus about 1 hour/month per practice in year 1, falling as templates mature (ASSUMPTION).
- **Automation:** the payment partner handles collection, retries and mandates. Reminders and statements are generated automatically.
- **Failure modes and responses:**
  - failed debits → the partner retries and the practice is notified
  - an owner disputes a charge → the settlement PDF serves as evidence
